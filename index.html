What Type Of Game to Start As A Developer

Designed to help aspiring game developers decide what type of game to start developing based on popular trends and audience engagement. By analyzing different game genres, peak player counts, and demographic preferences, this page provides insights into the types of games that resonate most with various audiences. Whether you are new to game development or looking for inspiration, this page will guide you towards making informed decisions about your first game project.

The global map visualization showcases that game popularity is not evenly distributed across regions. Countries with higher levels of internet access and gaming culture, such as North America and Western Europe, show significantly higher percentages of game popularity. Developing regions, such as parts of Africa and South Asia, have comparatively lower percentages, potentially due to barriers like internet availability and cultural differences.

Developers targeting new audiences should consider these regional differences when deciding which types of games to localize or promote in specific areas. Accessibility and infrastructure support should also be taken into account.

The bar chart highlights the all-time peak player counts for popular games. It is evident that online multiplayer and battle royale games have a massive appeal, with "PUBG: BATTLEGROUNDS" topping the list. The inclusion of games like "Counter-Strike 2" and "Dota 2" reflects a significant interest in both competitive and collaborative multiplayer experiences.

Developers looking to capture a large player base should focus on creating engaging, competitive online experiences, possibly incorporating multiplayer elements that promote replayability and community growth.

The pie chart shows the distribution of indie game developers by country, focusing on those countries with at least five developers. The United States stands out as having the most significant number of indie game developers, making up a large proportion of the total. Countries like Canada, the United Kingdom, Sweden, and the Czech Republic also have notable indie game developer communities. This distribution suggests that regions with robust game development industries and support systems foster larger indie game communities.

For aspiring developers, being aware of these regional concentrations can be helpful, as these countries may offer more resources, networking opportunities, and industry events that can support independent game development.

The chart shows that video game popularity is highest among younger audiences (16-44). These groups have the highest engagement, with Action Adventure, Sports, and Shooter genres being popular. Younger players also enjoy a diverse range of gaming experiences.

Comparing to that, for older groups (55-64 and 65+), there is a significant decline in popularity. The 55-64 group retains some interest, while the 65+ group prefers casual genres like Puzzle Platform and Online Board Games, favoring accessibility and slower-paced games.

This clearly indicates that video game popularity declines with age. Younger players (16-44) have the highest engagement across various genres, while older groups prefer less intense, strategic games.

Developers should focus on action-packed games for younger audiences and strategy games for older players.
